# Results (draft)

## R6. Conclusions

1. **Tuned Decision Tree performance against baselines on 2023 validation data.**
We asked whether a tuned Decision Tree improved upon Linear Regression and the route × hour median baseline when predicting delay duration on 2023 validation data (62,924 incidents). We tuned a 24-setting Decision Tree grid across `max_depth` and `min_samples_leaf` on 2014–2022 training data (683,475 rows), applied our pre-registered 0.02 min simplicity tie-breaker, and estimated 95% bootstrap intervals across 1,000 resamples (seed 42). In `results/tuning_tree.csv` and `results/baseline_table.csv`, the winning Decision Tree (`max_depth=None, min_samples_leaf=100`) achieved a 2023 validation MAE of 6.64 min (95% CI: 6.54–6.74; training MAE 5.58 min) and RMSE of 14.22 min (95% CI: 13.89–14.55), landing inside our pre-registered expectations (6.6–7.2 min MAE and 14.0–16.0 min RMSE in `docs/expected_results.md`). It beat the runner-up tree (`max_depth=20, min_samples_leaf=100`, MAE 6.72 min) by 0.08 min and outperformed the route × hour median (MAE 6.97 min, 95% CI: 6.83–7.09; RMSE 17.62 min), Linear Regression (MAE 7.71 min, 95% CI: 7.60–7.80; RMSE 14.62 min), and the global median (MAE 8.28 min, 95% CI: 8.14–8.42; RMSE 18.76 min) with non-overlapping 95% MAE intervals. These results are consistent with regularized nonlinear splits capturing interactions among route, hour, and incident type better than linear features or route × hour lookups, but they do not establish generalization to the held-out 2024 test year.

2. **Leaf-size regularization versus tree depth (sensitivity).**
We asked whether restricting tree depth or enforcing a minimum leaf size better controlled overfitting on noisy delay durations. We compared all 24 combinations in `results/tuning_tree.csv` across `max_depth` in {4, 8, 12, 16, 20, None} and `min_samples_leaf` in {1, 20, 100, 500}. With `min_samples_leaf=1`, growing deeper trees worsened 2023 validation MAE from 7.30 min (depth 12) to 9.10 min (no depth limit) while training MAE dropped to 1.11 min; in contrast, setting `min_samples_leaf=100` allowed unrestricted depth to improve validation MAE monotonically to 6.64 min and narrowed the train–validation gap from roughly 8 min to 1.06 min (5.58 vs. 6.64 min), whereas `min_samples_leaf=20` (6.85 min) and `500` (6.89 min) both degraded MAE by about 0.2 min. This pattern is consistent with minimum leaf size preventing memorization of individual outlier incidents without sacrificing deep feature interactions, though it does not show that `min_samples_leaf=100` would remain optimal under different features or an absolute-error split criterion.

3. **Training-window robustness and the MAE-versus-RMSE trade-off (Experiment B).**
We asked whether training only on recent post-2020 years outperformed the full 2014–2022 history given upward post-2020 delay drift (training mean 13.42 min vs. 16.78 min in 2023). Using `run_training_window.py`, we refit the tuned tree (`max_depth=None, min_samples_leaf=100`) on 2014–2022 (683,475 rows), 2018–2022 (321,959 rows), and 2020–2022 (163,476 rows) and compared them on the identical 2023 validation set using paired bootstrap intervals (`results/training_window.csv`, `results/experiment_log.csv`). Training on 2014–2022 achieved the lowest validation MAE (6.64 min, 95% CI: 6.54–6.74), beating 2018–2022 (6.70 min, 95% CI: 6.59–6.79; paired Δ +0.05 min, 95% CI: +0.02 to +0.08) and 2020–2022 (6.69 min, 95% CI: 6.59–6.79; paired Δ +0.05 min, 95% CI: +0.01 to +0.09), whereas 2020–2022 achieved the lowest validation RMSE (13.96 vs. 14.22 min). These findings are consistent with larger historical sample size modestly aiding typical-delay MAE while recent years better capture heavier tail delays for RMSE, though the roughly 3-second MAE difference is small in practice and does not guarantee which training window will perform best in 2024.

## R7. Limitations

- **Predictions apply only once a delay already exists.** All models estimate duration conditional on an incident having been logged, so they cant predict in advance whether a rider's trip will be delayed.
- **Validation-set optimism from tuning on 2023.** Because 2023 validation data guided hyperparameter selection across our tuning grid, reported 2023 error estimates are slightly optimistic relative to unseen future performance.
- **Trees split using squared error rather than absolute error.** Standard scikit-learn Decision Trees minimize squared error and output leaf means, which pulls predictions upward on right-skewed delays rather than directly minimizing our primary MAE metric.
- **Post-2020 data drift.** Ridership, schedules, and long-delay tails shifted after 2020 (with mean delay rising from 13.42 min in 2014–2022 to 16.78 min in 2023), meaning older training records reflect partially different operating conditions.
- **Location text was excluded.** Fine-grained station and intersection descriptions were not included as model features, leaving corridor- and bottleneck-specific delay patterns unmodeled.
- **Single city.** All records come exclusively from Toronto, so local traffic patterns, weather, and mixed-traffic streetcar dynamics limit generalization to other cities.
- **Single transit operator.** The dataset reflects only TTC operational logging conventions and incident taxonomies, limiting transferability to other transit agencies.
